# Pizza Sales Analysis — Business Insights & Recommendations

## Executive Summary

The Pizza Sales Analysis evaluates 48,620 pizza sales transactions to understand overall business performance, customer purchasing patterns, product performance, and sales trends across different time periods.

The analysis generated five core KPIs:

- **Total Revenue:** $817,860.05
- **Total Pizzas Sold:** 49,574
- **Total Orders:** 21,350
- **Average Order Value (AOV):** $38.31
- **Average Pizzas per Order:** 2.32

The results show relatively balanced performance across pizza categories, strong demand for large pizzas, higher sales toward the end of the week, and clear concentration of sales during the lunch and early evening periods.

---

## 1. Key Performance Indicators

| KPI | Result |
|---|---:|
| Total Revenue | $817,860.05 |
| Total Pizzas Sold | 49,574 |
| Total Orders | 21,350 |
| Average Order Value | $38.31 |
| Average Pizzas per Order | 2.32 |

### Insight

The business generated approximately **$817.9K in revenue** from **21,350 orders**, with customers purchasing an average of **2.32 pizzas per order**.

The AOV of **$38.31** provides a useful baseline for evaluating future promotions, upselling strategies, and changes in customer purchasing behavior.

## 2. Sales Performance by Pizza Category

| Category | Revenue | Share of Revenue |
|---|---:|---:|
| Classic | $220,053.10 | 26.91% |
| Supreme | $208,197.00 | 25.46% |
| Chicken | $195,919.50 | 23.96% |
| Veggie | $193,690.45 | 23.68% |

### Insight

**Classic pizzas generated the highest revenue**, contributing **26.91%** of total revenue.

Supreme pizzas followed with **25.46%**, while Chicken and Veggie contributed **23.96%** and **23.68%**, respectively.

The relatively narrow gap between the four categories indicates that revenue is **well distributed across categories**, rather than being heavily dependent on a single category.

### Business Implication

The business should continue supporting all major categories while giving additional promotional attention to the Classic category because it currently generates the largest revenue contribution.

Lower-performing categories should not necessarily be discontinued based on revenue alone, since the difference between categories is relatively small.

### Insight

# 10. Overall Business Insights

The analysis highlights several important patterns:

### 1. Classic pizzas are the strongest category

Classic generated the highest revenue and sold the highest number of pizzas, making it the strongest overall category in the dataset.

### 2. Large pizzas dominate revenue

Large pizzas generated approximately **45.89% of total revenue**, making them the most important size category for sales and inventory planning.

### 3. Sales increase toward the end of the week

Friday was the strongest sales day, while Sunday was the weakest.

### 4. Lunch is the strongest sales period

12 PM was the highest-revenue hour, with strong demand continuing into the early afternoon.

### 5. A small group of products drives a significant share of revenue

The five highest-revenue pizzas contributed approximately **24.53% of total revenue**, making them important products for promotions and inventory planning.

### 6. The product portfolio is relatively balanced by category

No single category dominates revenue overwhelmingly. The four categories each contribute between approximately 23.7% and 26.9% of revenue.

# 11. Recommendations

## 1. Prioritize high-performing products

Give greater promotional visibility to high-revenue pizzas such as:

- The Thai Chicken Pizza
- The Barbecue Chicken Pizza
- The California Chicken Pizza
- The Classic Deluxe Pizza
- The Spicy Italian Pizza

## 2. Optimize staffing around peak hours

Increase staffing and preparation capacity during the strongest periods, particularly around lunchtime and early evening.

## 3. Prepare for high-demand days

Friday shows the highest revenue and should receive additional attention in terms of staffing, ingredients, and operational preparation.

## 4. Optimize inventory for Large pizzas

Since Large pizzas account for approximately 45.89% of revenue, inventory planning should ensure sufficient ingredients and capacity to meet demand for this size.

## 5. Review low-performing products

The lowest-performing pizzas should be evaluated based on sales, pricing, customer demand, and ingredient costs before deciding whether to modify or discontinue them.

## 6. Use targeted promotions

Promotional campaigns can be tested during lower-performing periods, particularly on lower-revenue days and months, to determine whether demand can be increased.

## 7. Monitor KPIs regularly

The business should continuously monitor:

- Revenue
- Orders
- Pizzas sold
- Average Order Value
- Average Pizzas per Order

Regular monitoring can help identify changes in customer behavior and sales performance over time.

# Conclusion

The Pizza Sales Analysis provides a clear overview of sales performance across products, categories, sizes, and time periods.

The business generated **$817,860.05 in revenue from 21,350 orders**, with an average order value of **$38.31** and an average of **2.32 pizzas per order**.

Classic pizzas and Large-sized pizzas are particularly important revenue drivers, while Friday and the lunchtime period represent major sales opportunities.

The analysis suggests that the business can improve operational efficiency and sales performance by focusing inventory and staffing around peak demand, promoting high-performing products, and systematically reviewing low-performing products.
